Fernhollow: Garden Sort
A cosy shelf-sort puzzle game

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

The short version. Fernhollow has no accounts and never asks for your name, email or anything like it. Your progress stays on your own device, and we have no servers that receive it. The phone app shows ads from Google AdMob, which collects some information about your device to show and measure them; in the UK, EU and Switzerland it asks your permission first. If you buy anything in the game, Apple (on iPhone) or Google Play (on Android) handles the payment, and we never see your payment details.

Who makes Fernhollow

Fernhollow: Garden Sort ("Fernhollow", "the game") is made by Littlehollow Games, an independent developer, called "we" on this page. If you have any question about this policy or your information, email fernhollowgame@gmail.com.

Your game progress

Your levels, coins, Sprouts, boosters, restored garden centre, collections, purchases and settings are saved on your device only: in the game's own storage, with a second copy in the app's settings storage so a phone that's short of space doesn't lose it. They may also be included in your phone's own backups (for example iCloud or Google's Android backup), which we can't see. Deleting the app deletes them.

Moving to a new phone

Settings → Move to a new phone turns your progress into a code that you copy and type into the game on your new phone. The code holds only your game progress and settings, nothing about you. It never passes through us: it goes wherever you choose to put it (a note, a message to yourself), so keep it to yourself, as anyone with the code can copy your progress.

Ads in the phone app (Google AdMob)

The game is free because it shows ads: a short break ad now and then after you win a level, and optional video ads you can choose to watch for a reward. There are never ads in the middle of a level. These ads come from Google AdMob. To show them, Google may collect:

  • your device's advertising identifier: on iPhone only if you allow tracking when Apple's "Allow tracking?" message appears; on Android, unless you've deleted it in your phone's settings
  • your IP address, and a rough location worked out from it (country or city, never your precise location)
  • your device type, operating system, language and app version
  • which ads were shown, and whether they were watched or tapped
  • diagnostic information, such as how quickly ads load and whether the app closed unexpectedly while one was showing

Google uses this to show ads, count them, prevent fraud and, only where you've given permission, choose ads that suit you. Google may handle this information outside the UK and EU, including in the United States. You can read how in how Google uses information from apps that use its services and in Google's Privacy Policy.

Your choices:

  • In the UK, the EU and Switzerland, a message from Google asks for your consent before any personal data is used for ads, with a button to say no. You can change your answer at any time in the game's Settings → Privacy choices.
  • If you say no, you'll still see ads, but they won't be chosen using your personal data. Google may still use limited information to show and count them and to prevent fraud.
  • If the game can't check your choices (for example with no internet), it shows no ads until it can.
  • On iPhone, you can change your tracking choice at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • On Android, you can delete your advertising ID at any time in your phone's Settings → Privacy → Ads (on some phones, Settings → Google → Ads).

We ask Google for ads suitable for all ages only.

Why this is allowed: ads chosen using your personal data rely on your consent, which you can withdraw at any time. Showing and counting ads, and preventing fraud, rely on the legitimate interest in keeping the game free and the ads honest.

Purchases

The phone app offers optional purchases: Remove Ads (switches off the break ads for good), a one-off Starter Pack, packs of coins, and an Event Pass for each seasonal event. Every level can be won without buying anything. On iPhone, Apple handles the payment through the App Store; on Android, Google handles it through Google Play. We never see your card or payment details, your name or your email. The game only learns from Apple or Google which product was bought, and a reference number for the purchase, so it can add what you paid for exactly once and bring back Remove Ads and the Starter Pack after Restore purchases. See Apple's Privacy Policy and Google's Privacy Policy.

Morning reminders

If you turn on the morning reminder, the game asks your phone to show a short note at 9 in the morning. It's set up and shown by your phone itself: nothing is sent to us or anyone else. You can turn it off in the game's Settings or in your phone's notification settings.

Photos and sharing

The camera button takes a picture of your garden centre. It's saved or shared only when you choose to, through your phone's own share options, to whichever app or person you pick. "Tell a friend" in Settings does the same with a link to the game's store page, and "Rate Fernhollow" opens the game's page in the App Store or Google Play. We don't receive any of these.

Emails to us

If you email us, we'll see your email address and whatever you write, and use them only to answer you. We delete support emails once they're no longer needed to help you, and within two years at most.

Playing in a web browser

When you play Fernhollow on a games website such as Poki, that website shows the ads and its own privacy policy applies to them. There are no purchases, and the game saves your progress only in your browser's storage on your device.

Children

Fernhollow is made for a general audience and isn't aimed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect personal information from anyone, children included. If you think a child's information has been shared with us, email us and we'll help.

Your rights

Under UK and EU data protection law you can ask to see, correct or delete personal information about you, and object to how it's used. As we don't keep any personal information ourselves apart from emails you send us, there's normally nothing for us to hand over, but we're happy to answer questions. For ad data, use the choices above or contact Google. If you're unhappy with how your information is handled, you can complain to the UK's Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or to the data protection authority where you live.

Changes to this policy

If the game changes in a way that affects your information (for example a new feature or ad partner), we'll update this page and the date at the top.
